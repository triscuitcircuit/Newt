A native Godot 4 OpenXR demo for Mac: one small island, three stations,
built entirely in code from primitives — no imported assets. It exists to
show what Mac-native VR through OpenXR (Godot's Vulkan renderer on a local
runtime such as the Monado Quest Link driver) can do today, and to exercise
the Newt session-manager handoff end to end.

For the minimal starter project, see \href{../godot}{\texttt{../godot}}. This is the
bigger sibling.
\section{What's in the scene}
\label{sec:org600d2dc}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The Atrium} — emissive crystal spire, counter-rotating metallic rings,
a GPU particle fountain, procedural sky, and a day/dusk lighting toggle
with animated transitions.
\item \textbf{Physics Yard} — a crate pyramid, bouncy balls, and a seesaw plank.
Grab any prop with the grip, carry it, throw it with real hand velocity.
\item \textbf{Target Range} — five floating targets on animated paths that speed up
as you score, ray shooting with haptic pulses and generated sound effects.
\item \textbf{Control pedestal} — physical 3D buttons: Spawn Ball, Reset Physics,
Dusk/Day, and Teleport on/off.
\item \textbf{Everywhere} — 140-instance MultiMesh scatter in one draw call, dynamic
shadows, ACES tonemapping with glow, fog, and a wrist HUD with live
score/shots/FPS.
\end{itemize}
\section{Controls}
\label{sec:org6cd4aa2}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Input & Action\\
\hline
Left thumbstick & Smooth move (or nothing in teleport mode)\\
Right thumbstick ↔ & Snap turn 45°\\
Right thumbstick ↑ (teleport mode) & Aim teleport arc, release to jump\\
Trigger & Shoot targets / press 3D buttons\\
Grip (near a prop) & Grab · release to throw\\
Menu button & Pause panel: Resume, or Quit to Newt\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Quitting follows the Newt cooperative convention: the game exits its
process and the session manager restores the Newt dashboard.

Desktop preview (no headset): the camera slowly orbits the island.
\texttt{Esc} opens the pause panel, \texttt{Q} quits from it.
\section{Run it}
\label{sec:org05fc55a}
Requirements match the \href{../godot}{minimal example}: Godot 4.4 with OpenXR +
Vulkan on macOS, a compatible separately-supplied OpenXR runtime, matching
architectures, and an awake headset with no other app owning the session.

\textbf{Editor Play (recommended):} open this folder in Godot, configure Newt
once via \textbf{Project → Tools → Configure Newt…}, press Play. The bundled
Newt Play Bridge starts/reuses the session manager, reserves the headset,
and restores the dashboard on Stop. See \href{../../docs/GODOT-PLAY.md}{\texttt{docs/GODOT-PLAY.md}}.

\textbf{Session manager CLI:}
\begin{verbatim}
# After building Newt (or use the installed `newt` command):
../../build/newtd run --project "$PWD"
\end{verbatim}

\textbf{Direct launcher (no Newt):}
\begin{verbatim}
python3 launch.py --runtime /absolute/path/to/runtime.json --godot /path/to/Godot
\end{verbatim}
\section{Validate without a headset}
\label{sec:orga5421be}
\begin{verbatim}
godot --headless --path . --import
godot --headless --path . -s tests/showcase_smoke.gd
\end{verbatim}
The smoke test instantiates the real scene in desktop-preview mode and
drives every pedestal action, a scoring shot, and the pause flow. It
validates scripts and scene assembly only. OpenXR initialization,
controller bindings, frame timing, USB transport, and comfort still need a
hardware run — that part is honestly unverified until someone puts the
headset on.
\section{Layout}
\label{sec:orga7e8989}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
File & Role\\
\hline
\texttt{main.gd} & World building, stations, scoring, pause, day/dusk\\
\texttt{scripts/player\_rig.gd} & Hands, rays, locomotion, teleport, grabbing, HUD\\
\texttt{scripts/factory.gd} & Materials, props, 3D buttons, signs\\
\texttt{scripts/sound\_bank.gd} & Procedural WAV sound effects (no assets)\\
\texttt{tests/showcase\_smoke.gd} & Headless interaction smoke test\\
\texttt{addons/newt\_bridge/} & Newt Play Bridge (installed, do not edit here)\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Godot owns its OpenXR session here — \texttt{Newt\_Run()} is never called inside
the game. Newt's role is the dashboard/launcher around it and the
one-owner handoff, exactly as in the minimal example.
